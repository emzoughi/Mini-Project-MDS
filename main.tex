\subsection*{Q1.1 -- Kinematics}

The CoreXY mechanism uses two motors to control the position of the print head in the $x$-$y$ plane. We assume that the belts are inextensible, that there is no slipping between the belts and pulleys, and that both driving pulleys have the same radius $r_m$.

Let $\theta_l$ and $\theta_r$ denote the angular positions of the left and right motors respectively. The linear displacement of a belt driven by a pulley is given by $s=r_m\theta$.

Due to the CoreXY belt arrangement, horizontal motion requires both motors to rotate in the same direction, whereas vertical motion requires them to rotate in opposite directions. Choosing consistent positive directions for the motor angles and Cartesian axes, the belt displacement constraints are

\begin{equation}
\begin{cases}
r_m\Delta\theta_l = \Delta x + \Delta y,\\
r_m\Delta\theta_r = \Delta x - \Delta y.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}

Adding and subtracting these equations gives

\begin{equation}
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle \Delta x = \frac{r_m}{2}
(\Delta\theta_l+\Delta\theta_r),\\[6pt]
\displaystyle \Delta y = \frac{r_m}{2}
(\Delta\theta_l-\Delta\theta_r).
\end{cases}
\end{equation}

Taking the origin of the Cartesian coordinate system as the reference position corresponding to $\theta_l=\theta_r=0$, the print head coordinates become

\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
x &= \frac{r_m}{2}(\theta_l+\theta_r),\\
y &= \frac{r_m}{2}(\theta_l-\theta_r).
\end{aligned}
}
\end{equation}

Differentiating with respect to time yields the corresponding velocities

\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\dot{x} &= \frac{r_m}{2}
(\dot{\theta}_l+\dot{\theta}_r),\\
\dot{y} &= \frac{r_m}{2}
(\dot{\theta}_l-\dot{\theta}_r).
\end{aligned}
}
\end{equation}

These kinematic relations can also be written in matrix form

\begin{equation}
\begin{bmatrix}
\dot{x}\\
\dot{y}
\end{bmatrix}
=
\frac{r_m}{2}
\begin{bmatrix}
1 & 1\\
1 & -1
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}
\dot{\theta}_l\\
\dot{\theta}_r
\end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}

\subsection*{Q1.2 -- Dynamics}
\label{sec:q12}

\paragraph{Electrical dynamics.}
For each motor $j\in\{l,r\}$, we chose to neglect the back electromotive force. Kirchhoff's voltage law and the electromechanical torque relation give
\begin{equation}
  u_{m,j}=L_m\dot i_{m,j}+R_m i_{m,j},
  \qquad \tau_j=K_m i_{m,j}.
  \label{eq:q12_electrical}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Mechanical dynamics.}
Using the kinematic relations derived in Q1.1,
\begin{equation}
  \dot x=\frac{r_m}{2}(\dot\theta_l+\dot\theta_r),\qquad
  \dot y=\frac{r_m}{2}(\dot\theta_l-\dot\theta_r),
  \label{eq:q12_kinematics}
\end{equation}
the total kinetic energy of the two motor inertias and the print head is
\begin{align}
  K &= \frac{J_m}{2}(\dot\theta_l^2+\dot\theta_r^2)
       +\frac{m}{2}(\dot x^2+\dot y^2)\\
    &=\frac{J_{\mathrm{eff}}}{2}(\dot\theta_l^2+\dot\theta_r^2),
  \qquad J_{\mathrm{eff}}=J_m+\frac{m r_m^2}{2}.
  \label{eq:q12_kinetic}
\end{align}

\paragraph{Bearing friction.}
We define the Rayleigh dissipation as $D=\frac12\sum_j\mu_j v_j^2$, where $v_j$ denotes the tangential speed of a rolling bearing relative to its axis, or the sliding speed of a linear bearing. The mechanism contains two stationary-axis upper bearings on each belt, four lower bearings whose axes travel with the horizontal gantry, two $x$-direction linear bearings and two $y$-direction linear bearings. We assume that bearings of a given type have the same coefficient.

The two upper bearings on the left belt have tangential speeds of magnitude $|r_m\dot\theta_l|$, and the two on the right belt have $|r_m\dot\theta_r|$. For each lower bearing the tangential speed relative to its moving axis is $|\dot x|$, a purely vertical gantry translation does not make these bearings spin. Each linear bearing travels with speed $|\dot x|$ or $|\dot y|$ respectively. Consequently,
\begin{equation}
  D =
  \mu_r r_m^2(\dot\theta_l^2+\dot\theta_r^2)
  +(2\mu_r+\mu_{l,x})\dot x^2+\mu_{l,y}\dot y^2.
  \label{eq:q12_dissipation}
\end{equation}
